The Ultimate NZ Car Service Checklist:
What You Really Need (and When)

Keeping your car in top condition isn’t just about avoiding breakdowns — it’s about safety, reliability, and saving money in the long run. In New Zealand, where our roads range from smooth highways to rugged backroads, regular car servicing is essential.

By Stacey Bree, Franchise Network Manager

Published [DD Month YYYY]

Key takeaways

  • Service your car every 12 months or [10,000-15,000] km, whichever comes first.
  • Alternate minor and major services. Minor covers oil, filter and safety checks.
  • NZ law requires at least 1.5 mm of tyre tread across the tread pattern.

With so many maintenance schedules and recommendations, it can be hard to know exactly what your car really needs and when. That’s why we’ve created the ultimate NZ car service checklist to help Kiwi drivers stay on top of their vehicle care.

Regular servicing matters because cars are complex machines. Over time, parts wear down, fluids degrade, and small issues can escalate into costly repairs if left unchecked. A properly serviced car runs smoother, consumes less fuel, and keeps both you and your passengers safe. In New Zealand’s diverse driving conditions, staying ahead of maintenance is critical for avoiding inconvenient breakdowns and protecting your investment.

How often should I service my car in NZ?

When it comes to servicing, engine oil and fluids are the lifeblood of your vehicle. Engine oil should be replaced regularly according to manufacturer recommendations, while brake fluid, coolant, and transmission fluid all need routine checks to ensure optimal performance. Brakes themselves require careful attention, as worn pads, discs, or handbrakes can compromise safety and are a common reason for vehicles failing their WOF. Tyres are another crucial area to monitor; tread depth, sidewall condition, and correct tyre pressure not only improve handling but are also a legal requirement in New Zealand.

Lights and electrical systems play a huge role in vehicle safety. Headlights, indicators, brake lights, hazard lights, and the battery should all be tested regularly to avoid roadside issues. Steering and suspension components, including shocks, struts, and tie rods, should be inspected for wear or looseness, as these parts impact both safety and driving comfort. Visibility is also vital, so wiper blades, windscreens, and windows should always be in good condition, with chips or cracks repaired promptly to maintain clear sightlines.

Filters, belts, and hoses are often overlooked but play a critical role in keeping your car running efficiently. Air, cabin, and fuel filters protect your engine and passengers from dust and debris, while belts and hoses ensure mechanical systems function properly. Exhaust and emissions systems should be monitored for leaks or rust, maintaining both performance and compliance with environmental standards. Finally, interior safety features like seat belts, mirrors, and the horn should be in perfect working order to provide protection in any situation.

What is a brake service?

A brake service is an inspection and, where needed, repair or replacement of your vehicle’s braking components, including brake pads, discs or rotors, brake fluid and related parts. Worn brakes are a common cause of WOF failures and a key safety issue. Pit Stop’s technicians can inspect your brakes, advise what work is required and carry out the repairs at all Pit Stop branches across New Zealand.

How much does a brake service cost?

Estimates for vehicle repairs and replacement parts can be obtained from your local branch ahead of booking in the work, so we recommend contacting your local branch to discuss the specifics of the work to be completed.

How long will it take to get my brakes done?

Depending on the details of the work needed, Brake work can sometimes be done on a “while you wait” basis, while for bigger jobs it is best to leave your car with us. A courtesy car can sometimes be made available, so it is best to discuss this with the branch when you book.

Does Pit Stop provide courtesy vehicles?

Yes, many Pit Stop branches can provide a courtesy vehicle if your repair or service is likely to take longer than is convenient to wait. Contact your nearest Pit Stop branch to check whether a courtesy vehicle is available.
